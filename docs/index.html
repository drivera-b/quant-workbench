Research snapshot

The project is strongest when it reports limitations directly, separates signal quality from payout geometry, and keeps recent performance anchored to a frozen benchmark.

Why it exists

This project was built to behave more like a research system than a backtest showcase. It treats strategy evaluation as a layered process with explicit failure modes.

Research question

Some opening-session event families in equity-index futures appear structured enough to study objectively. The goal is not to claim easy alpha. The goal is to test whether modest intraday edges can survive realistic friction and still make sense after account constraints are modeled.

What the architecture separates

Signal discovery Context features Trade-level EV Payout geometry Execution drift Forward validation

What survived deeper scrutiny

The project became more credible as it stopped promoting every promising branch and started rewarding the branches that held up under stronger checks.

Strengths

  • Frozen benchmark instead of endless retuning
  • Recent-window tracking against historical OOS
  • Lifecycle simulation under multiple firm rules
  • Stress scenarios for costs and fill quality
  • Clear public-safe framing for recruiters

Known weak spots

  • Execution quality matters a lot for short holding periods
  • Month-to-month performance is uneven
  • Live unattended automation is not fully proven
  • Recent sample is encouraging, not a guarantee
  • Venue adapter quality can make or break deployment

Validation posture

The benchmark was frozen first. Newer samples were then judged on whether they stayed broadly consistent with that benchmark instead of being used as an excuse to keep tuning.

Monthly stability

The project is honest about instability: April was weak, May was strong, and the newer sample stayed positive but not uniformly smooth.

Benchmark discipline

  • Recent data is compared to a locked benchmark
  • Policy change is explicit: exclude one weak event family
  • Observed sample is judged by EV bands, not hype
  • Current status: research-based go, not execution-proven go

What would invalidate it

  • Execution drift large enough to resemble the 5% haircut stress
  • Recent windows turning persistently negative
  • Signal cadence collapsing versus historical assumptions
  • Venue adapter failing to reproduce the benchmark intents

How it can be used

The public repository is useful as a lightweight research workbench for anyone who has an empirical trade CSV and wants a quick read on trade-level expectancy, uncertainty, and simple lifecycle economics.

Useful for

  • quant research demos and portfolio projects
  • evaluating a backtest trade export without a full stack
  • teaching trade-level EV versus account-level geometry
  • sanity-checking whether a trade stream survives friction assumptions

Not intended as

  • a turnkey live trading system
  • a broker-connected execution engine
  • a public release of private alpha configuration
  • a substitute for venue-specific execution validation

Stress and payout geometry

The useful result here is not that the system has a pretty base case. It is that we can see exactly which friction assumptions it tolerates and which ones break it first.

Interpretation

  • Small extra costs do not destroy the profile
  • Broad fill degradation is much more damaging
  • Execution quality matters more than cosmetic optimization
  • That makes venue choice and automation quality first-order issues

Why this matters

The project is not just a signal engine. It is also a study of how constraints, resets, payout timing, and fee drag reshape the economics of a small edge.

The strongest public-facing lesson is not “I found a magic strategy.” It is “I built a system that can tell me when a promising branch is still not good enough.”
